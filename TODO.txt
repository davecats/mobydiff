1024^3 strong-scaling comparison, seconds per timestep:

GPUs | MPI dims | mobyDiff | AMPHIBIOUS | AMPHIBIOUS/mobyDiff
-----|----------|----------|------------|--------------------
4    | 1 2 2    | 0.714    | 0.617      | 0.86
8    | 2 2 2    | 0.386    | 0.406      | 1.05
16   | 2 2 4    | 0.255    | 0.258      | 1.01
32   | 2 4 4    | 0.139    | 0.184      | 1.32
64   | 4 4 4    | 0.137    | 0.150      | 1.09

Notes:
- mobyDiff is slightly slower on 4 GPUs, but matches or outperforms AMPHIBIOUS from 8 GPUs upward.
- Both codes show a plateau from 32 to 64 GPUs, suggesting a hardware/runtime communication limit may be important for this case size.
- Nsight Systems showed healthy scaling of the main GPU kernels. The dominant kernels are the red-black pressure sweep and momentum predictor.
- Remaining optimisation candidates, when needed:
  * reduce OpenMP target-region synchronisation in the pressure loop;
  * consider merging or restructuring small kernels such as apply_bc around the red-black sweep;
  * add explicit internal timers for halo exchange/MPI wait time, since Nsight did not collect MPI events;
  * consider interior/boundary box splitting and communication/computation overlap only if MPI wait is confirmed significant;
  * clean up initialization-time host-to-device transfers with map(alloc:) where arrays are initialized directly on the GPU.
- For now, performance is good enough to move focus to validation cases and utilities for setting up/initialising flow cases.

Autotuning notes from 2026-05-31
================================

Autotuning could be useful for production runs, especially multi-node cases, but it should remain outside the solver core. The solver should continue to read a simple explicit decomposition from input.ini:

[mpi]
dims = nxr nyr nzr

The autotuning utility can generate and test those values.

Candidate tuning targets:
- MPI Cartesian decomposition for a fixed global grid and rank count.
  * Try all reasonable factorizations of the rank count, e.g. for 32 ranks: 1 4 8, 2 4 4, 4 4 2, etc.
  * Use a simple surface-area communication model to order/prune candidates, but rely on measurements for the final choice.
  * The best decomposition may depend on physical boundary conditions, local block shape, red-black halo frequency, GPU-aware MPI behaviour, and network topology.
- MPI launch/runtime settings.
  * Compare a small set of robust launcher options, e.g. --bind-to none, --bind-to core, ppr:4:node, and PE=1/2 variants.
  * Compare a few MPI presets through MPI_EXTRA_ARGS, e.g. default, --mca pml ucx, and --mca pml ucx --mca coll_hcoll_enable 0.
  * Avoid aggressive UCX_TLS or low-level transport settings unless recommended by the cluster documentation, because they can silently disable good transports.

Suggested utility layout:

profiling/autotune/
  generate_decompositions.py
  run_autotune.slurm
  collect_autotune.py

Suggested workflow:
1. Generate candidate decompositions for nx, ny, nz, and ranks.
2. Generate temporary input.ini files with output disabled.
3. Run each candidate for a short fixed number of timesteps, e.g. 10-20.
4. Repeat candidates 2-3 times to reduce noise.
5. Store seconds_per_step, rank count, nodes, dims, MPI preset, launch flags, environment, and exit code.
6. Produce a sorted CSV/table with the best-performing candidates.

Example output table:

ranks | dims  | MPI preset | seconds_per_step | relative
------|-------|------------|------------------|---------
32    | 2 4 4 | default    | 0.139            | 1.00
32    | 4 4 2 | default    | 0.146            | 1.05
32    | 2 4 4 | hcoll_off  | 0.136            | 0.98

Guiding principle: autotune the scripts and input files, not the solver logic. Keep mobyDiff clean and explicit, while allowing hardware-specific optimisation through the profiling harness.
